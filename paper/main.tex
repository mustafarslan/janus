\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Mustafa Arslan}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Agentic large language models (LLMs) now move money through tools, yet the
record of what they did is usually a trace their own process emits beside the
effect. \emph{Janus} puts the record on the effect path. A step's proposal, the
verdict on it and any approval from a validator or a person, signed by whoever
gave or relayed it, are durable in a signed, hash-chained log before the step may
run; gates are pure functions of
that log; and an auditor re-derives every verdict offline from the log and one
public key. On the MCP edge the effect is held until then; on the SDK path, which
our model experiment uses, a cooperating client runs it only afterwards. We
describe the design, its invariants and a saga engine whose recorded histories
keep their meaning across rule changes, and evaluate it under crash injection
(144 kills in-process, 81 through the daemon), by verifying a 100-million-event
log offline (254.5\,s), and with a real model behind a lending workflow, run
governed and plain on the same recorded model outputs. With the lending mandate
in the model's prompt, the comparison was 0 against 0. With it only in the
policy, the model approved six out-of-mandate loans, three with no injection; the
plain agent paid all six and Janus none, each refused by a deterministic
validator and re-derivable offline. An always-approve oracle over the recorded
intakes gave 20 and 21 against 0. The later runs' hypotheses and predicted
counts were committed, with the harness, before they ran. Designing the experiment exposed, in a system
that passed its own audit, an instance of post-approval substitution: an approval
keyed to an attempt was counted for a different proposal, moving a person's
approval from 100 to 1{,}000{,}000. We report it, the fix, and what Janus does
not guarantee.
\end{abstract}
